What is where

The packages of Go for Meta Quest, the programs and tools it comes with, what you need to build for the headset, and what was checked: so far, only on a computer.

What is where

Piece What it does
app The program loop. Run starts Go Renderer’s headset activity (android.RunHeadset: no window, an OpenGL ES 3 context on its own thread), opens the OpenXR session there (openxr.Open), and goes round: it reads the session’s news; while the session does not run (the headset off the head, starting, paused) it sleeps 10 ms; while it runs it begins a frame, reads the controllers (only while the program has them), calls the program’s step, and ends the frame. It stops when the runtime asks the program to leave; when the step returns false or a frame fails it first asks the runtime to stop the session (RequestExit) and hands back empty frames until it has, two seconds at most. It closes the session on the drawing thread. Each Frame carries the input state (the controllers as a game pad), the head’s pose and whether it is tracked, both hands, the seconds since the last frame (from the display times, at most a tenth of a second), whether the program has the controllers, whether the frame will be shown, the two eyes (pose, field of view, framebuffer), whether those encode sRGB, and OpenGL ES. Options.Panels asks for panels by their size in pixels (PanelSize, at most openxr.MostPanels, checked before any is made); they are made when the session opens and every Frame.Panels holds them in that order, to place (Pose, FitWidth, Shown) and draw into (Target, only when the picture changes). Frame.Vibrate(hand, amplitude, duration) shakes a controller.
cmd/clear The first program: each eye cleared to a colour of its own going slowly round the colour wheel, the right eye half a turn behind. No renderer: it checks the package, the loader, the session and the pictures with nothing else in the way.
cmd/cube A lit 30 cm cube 1.5 m ahead of where the player first looked, at the head’s height, that stays there in the room; a 5 cm cube in each tracked controller’s grip. Drawn by Go Renderer’s gles.SceneRenderer.DrawViews: the frame planned once from a lens covering both eyes (camera.CoveringLens), then drawn in each eye with its own lopsided projection.
cmd/room The cube of cmd/cube in the player’s own room: it opens the session with Options.Passthrough, and while Frame.ShowsRoom it draws with gles.SceneRenderer.SeeThrough, so the headset’s cameras show the room wherever the cube is not. The first step of mixed reality; on a headset without passthrough it is the cube on black.
explore Walks a player through any scene.Scene at room scale: Run(world, Options) is the whole program loop. camera.RigWalk walks the rig with the left stick the way the head looks and turns it in steps around the head with the right one, the floor on the scene’s walkable ground under the head (world.GroundHeight). Options.WalkMetresSecond walks faster, and Options.TurnRadiansSecond turns smoothly about the head instead of in steps (a program whose right stick scrolls a panel feeds it 0 sideways meanwhile). Y or B switches between a person (scale 1) and a giant (GiantScale, 20 by default), the head kept over the same point of the ground. The near plane grows with the scale, Far is the scene’s; a cube in each tracked hand, as in cmd/cube. Options.Start is where the room stands at the start: the program decides. A frame’s steering makes no garbage (tested); the views are made in the same slices every frame. Show explores another scene (the hands go with the player); Move puts the room somewhere else in the same one, at any scale. A program with its own loop (panels, say) calls New, then Steer, Follow and Views every frame instead of Run.
place Where things stand, as sums on poses, tested anywhere. In the scene: RigWithHeadAt puts the head over a point of the floor looking a way, at a scale; TurnedAround and RescaledAround turn or resize the person about their head, which stays over the same point; RoomPoint is a point of the scene in the room and Keeping moves the rig so one is at a point of the room; TurnedAbout and ResizedAbout turn or resize a model about its middle; ModelAhead puts a scene on a table ahead of the head; StandingBefore sets the person down before a point, on their side, facing it. In the room: Ahead (a panel called up), FacingHead (a label), LeaningBack and UnderEyes (a strip low ahead, leaned back toward the eyes), Following (a yaw that follows the head only once it looks well away), PalmFacing and OverPalm (a menu over a hand whose palm is turned to the face), HeightForAngle; and HeadYaw, YawFacing, AngleBetween, AnglesOf.
mirror A finger on a picture of the headset’s screen points as the right controller does. Listen reads lines from a Unix socket (@name is Android’s abstract namespace, reached with adb forward): down, move and up with a point of the left eye’s picture, look with two (two fingers dragged), mark with one or off. Touches hands them to the frames one change at a time (a quick tap still presses and lets go), Hand is the controller the finger is, Carry carries out a drag (a panel it began on swings about the eye; elsewhere it says how far to turn the person), MarkPose places the mark, and Direction and PicturePoint go between the eye’s picture and the room.
selftest Goes through a program as a person would, with controllers of its own. A Runner runs Steps (ready, act, check), each settling a while before its check: PointAt points the right hand from beside the head at a point of the room and pulls the trigger once, Press presses a button for a frame, PressMenu the menu, HoldPalmUp turns the left palm to the face. A program calls Drive before its frame and Check after; in the headset it reads the runner’s Pad instead of the real controllers. AskedFor tells whether a file was put in the program’s folder with adb, to run it there.
cmd/explorer Go Renderer’s sample island (sample.Island(), its afternoon light and fog) explored with explore. The player starts on the ground twelve metres south of the village square, facing it, at a person’s size.
pointer Points at panels with a controller, as a laser. FromAim is the ray along a hand’s aim (-Z); HitPanel finds the pixel it meets on a panel, from the side it faces, and how far. A Pointer feeds an interface’s own input.State from one hand each frame: the cursor to that pixel, the trigger as the left button (pressed past 0.7, let go under 0.3), the stick up and down as the wheel (ScrollNotchesPerSecond at full tilt); when the ray leaves the panel a held button is let go and the cursor leaves the interface. The state’s window is the panel’s picture. PointAtNearest points one hand at several Interfaces (a panel, its input and its pointer) at once: the nearest shown one the ray meets has it, the others let go. SceneRay is the hand’s ray in the scene, through the rig; AimAt aims a hand of a program’s own at a point; PanelPoint is a pixel of a panel in the room. A Beam is a thin unlit bar from the hand to what it meets, placed through the rig, hidden on an untracked hand. Pointing makes no garbage (tested).
cmd/panel A board of six tiles floating 1.5 m ahead over the sample island, a panel drawn by gles.FlatRenderer (linear colour): the right controller’s beam points at it, the tile under it is outlined with a light buzz, the trigger lights a tile or puts it out with a stronger one. The board is drawn again only when it changes; the sticks walk the island while it stays with you.
tools/AndroidManifest.xml The manifest every package gets: Android’s NativeActivity loading libmain.so, the com.oculus.intent.category.VR category (immersive, not a flat window), head tracking required, com.oculus.supportedDevices (Quest 3 and 3S), com.oculus.vr.focusaware on the activity (the program keeps running, shown, while the system’s menu is up), landscape, kept out of the recent list, never recreated by a configuration change (the session holds the activity), one at a time, no Java. It names the OpenXR permissions and the broker’s providers the loader asks which runtime to load.
tools/fetch-loader.sh Reads Maven Central’s newest org.khronos.openxr:openxr_loader_for_android, downloads it, and keeps its arm64 libopenxr_loader.so and its licence in third_party/openxr-loader/ (not committed). A version can be named: tools/fetch-loader.sh 1.1.65.
tools/build-apk.sh Builds a program into build/<name>.apk, from the folder it runs in: go build -buildmode=c-shared with the NDK’s clang and 16 KB pages, the manifest linked by aapt2 against the newest platform’s android.jar, the program’s library and the loader stored uncompressed under lib/arm64-v8a/, aligned to 16 KB (zipalign -P 16), signed with the debug key (made if missing). Its id is io.github.akrck02.metaquest.<name> and its label the folder’s name, unless APK_ID and APK_LABEL say otherwise: a program in a module of its own builds with the same script, under its own id.
tools/install.sh Installs a package on the headset with adb install -r, starts it (by APK_ID when it was built with one), and shows the log under the tags Go and OpenXR.

What is not here is Go Renderer’s, as on the 3DS: the drawing (gles), the activity (android), the OpenXR session, its swapchains and the controllers (openxr), the poses and the eyes’ projections (graphics), the rig and the covering lens (camera).

What you need

  • Go 1.25 or later.
  • The Android SDK’s build tools (aapt2, zipalign with -P, so 35 or later; apksigner) and a platform, in ANDROID_HOME; the NDK in ANDROID_NDK_HOME. On a Mac, Homebrew’s android-commandlinetools is found without them.
  • Go Renderer’s headset pieces, which go.mod fetches by itself: a commit of its feature/openxr branch, until they are released.
  • adb, and a Meta Quest 3 or 3S in developer mode.
tools/build-apk.sh ./cmd/cube
tools/install.sh build/cube.apk

What was checked

On a Meta Quest 3 (Android 14, API 34, connected with adb in developer mode), from the system’s own log and the headset’s screen captures (adb exec-out screencap -p, both eyes side by side):

  • Both packages start immersive. The loader finds the runtime, and the session goes IDLE → READY → SYNCHRONIZED → VISIBLE → FOCUSED within a tenth of a second.
  • cmd/clear: each eye shows its own colour, the right eye half a turn behind on the colour wheel. With the system’s menu open the session drops to VISIBLE and the program keeps drawing under it; going home stops the session (STOPPING → IDLE) and the program waits without spinning.
  • cmd/cube: the cube and both hands’ cubes are drawn in each eye, over the horizon’s colour, with near things (the hands) further apart between the eyes than far ones, as they should be.
  • Frames keep the headset’s pace: 72 a second, none stale, the program’s processor time under 1 ms a frame (the runtime’s own VrApi line in adb logcat).
  • cmd/room: not checked on a headset. It builds for Android (its library compiles with the passthrough’s C); whether the room shows behind the cube needs the headset.
  • cmd/explorer: the island is drawn in each eye at 72 frames a second, the program’s processor time under 1 ms a frame, seen through the headset’s screen recording (adb exec-out screenrecord) with the headset lying on a desk, so without the sticks.
  • cmd/panel: the board is shown over the island, its own colours (not washed out) and its title readable, at 72 frames a second, with the haptics server connected and no error in the log. The headset lay on a desk: pointing, clicking and the buzzes are not tried with hands yet.

With the headset on, the cube was seen working as it should. Leaving from the system’s menu (Quit) is not tried yet.

On a Mac (Go 1.27, NDK 29, build tools 37, platform android-37, OpenXR loader 1.1.65):

  • go vet ./... and go test ./... pass: the loop’s decisions (leave, wait, frame), the clock (the first frame, 72 frames a second, a long pause, time going back, a restart), the eyes’ colours, the cube’s world (its size, faces pointing out, placed ahead at eye height and staying there, the hands’ cubes following tracked hands only), the island’s start (on the ground south of the square), and the exploration’s (the options’ defaults, the giant and back with the head over the same point, Y waiting for a tracked head, a second of the stick walking 1.5 m on the ground, the views and their covering lens, no allocations while steering).
  • go vet for Android (arm64, with the NDK’s clang) passes on every package.
  • tools/fetch-loader.sh downloads the loader; tools/build-apk.sh builds build/clear.apk, build/cube.apk and build/explorer.apk. Each holds the manifest, libmain.so and libopenxr_loader.so stored uncompressed; zipalign -c -P 16 and apksigner verify accept them, and both libraries’ segments are aligned to 16 KB.
  • On an Android 16 phone emulator (arm64), both packages install and start: the Go runtime runs main, the drawing thread gets its OpenGL ES 3 context, and the OpenXR loader starts and asks the runtime broker for a runtime. A phone has none, so the program ends there, cleanly, with openxr.instance-not-created in the log (adb logcat -s Go).

The manifest’s SDK levels (minimum 32, target 34) follow Meta’s documentation as last read; the headset above runs API 34.

Not yet

  • Walking, turning and the giant tried with the headset on.
  • Pointing at a panel, clicking and the buzzes, tried with the headset on; a Go UI interface on a panel (the pieces are there: pointer feeds an input.State as Go UI reads it).
  • The headset’s own settings: refresh rate, foveated rendering, passthrough.
  • Drawing both eyes in one pass (multiview), sound, storage.
  • A frame entirely without garbage: camera.CoveringLens (Go Renderer’s) still makes two small slices a frame, and explore cannot avoid them.
  • Programs on other headsets: the OpenXR part is standard, but the manifest is Meta’s.
